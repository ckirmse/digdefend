# Dig Defend — Build Milestones

Source: game design doc, exported to `gdd.txt` in this repo. Live version: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uSE6rXA2rIwvqYDbySOI4nr2OXErxfMbViEymtUqWaQ/edit (re-export with `curl -sL "https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uSE6rXA2rIwvqYDbySOI4nr2OXErxfMbViEymtUqWaQ/export?format=txt" -o gdd.txt`). Reference projects: `~/md` (dragons) and `~/square` (lights).

Each milestone should be testable and playable before moving to the next. Milestones are numbered `M<n>` in build order within a phase. Phases are roughly sequential, but later polish/meta phases can interleave once core gameplay (Phase 6) is playable.

Rules:
- Never mark a milestone complete or commit code without explicit approval.
- Every gameplay number lives in a `GameData` data-only module (checked into git for this project, unlike the reference games).
- Every clonable asset (rocks, monsters, weapons, tools, posts, defenses) lives in one central `ReplicatedStorage` location.
- Core loop first, art and polish later. A cube enemy is a fine enemy until Phase 9.
- When copying UI or systems from the reference games, restyle to this project's UI standards (fonts, capitalization, centering, sparse exclamation points).

Open design questions (sections of the GDD that are still empty headings, to be filled before their phase begins): Backpack, Boots, Posts, Defenses, Traps, Camp Level, Wave Cycle, Monsters, Random Events, Adjusting for Player Counts, Revives, Spectate Mode, Menus, Audio. Added 2026-10-03: Classes and Contracts have a direction in the GDD but every detail is still to be fleshed out (roster, odds, skills, XP curve, prices, tier curve, modifiers); see their "To be fleshed out" bullets. Revised the same day to four directly unlocked Classes with rolled, graded skills (21 at launch) instead of pulled Classes. Replaced 2026-10-05 by Characters (a generic character with a Diamonds reroll on its weapon, post, and three skills; more slots to buy), with Earning Diamonds and Contracts; all three still to be fleshed out.

**M55 — Character data and save shape** (needs fleshing out)
The per-character record in `PlayerSaveSchema` (level, XP, weapon and post type and stars, up to three skills with their rarities) and the list of character slots, with reset-contract coverage and replication. Single `StoreConfig` GameData file driving Featured, Weapons, Posts, Placements, and Currency sections. **M36 — 3D Store**

**M37 — Loadout Menu**
Owned/unowned/equipped states, equip/unequip/buy actions, slot replacement rules, five-slot loadout display. Loadout carried into the gameplay place via PlayerData.

**M56 — Character XP, stars, and the skill system** (needs fleshing out)
Character XP earned per run, slots 2 and 3 unlocking at character levels, weapon and post stars applied to their stats in a run, and the in-run skill system (first guess 36 skills in six families, each with a rarity, with a title for two of a family and a set bonus for three). **M57 — Character sheet and rerolls** (needs fleshing out)
The character sheet: five rows (weapon, post, skills 1 to 3), each with a Diamonds reroll. Weapon and post roll stars 1 to 5 on a type the player picks, shown beside the current one to keep either; a skill roll offers three to pick from or keep. Odds shown, and a direct-purchase path where `PolicyService` reports paid random items as restricted. Rank worked out from stars, rarities, and level. Needs the odds, reroll prices, and rank formula decided first.

**M38 — Diamond taps** (needs fleshing out)
The four ways Diamonds is earned (GDD "Earning Diamonds", structure copied from 99 Nights in the Forest): night milestones paid and saved at dawn so a lost run keeps them, Diamonds chests in the world, one-time badges that follow the completion rule, and each Contract's own rescue reward. Needs every amount decided first, set in rerolls per run. 2026-10-05: night milestones and the rescue reward were built in M32 (`DiamondTapManager`, placeholder amounts in `GameData/Run/DiamondTaps`), so what is left here is chests and badges, each Contract's own reward, and the real amounts. (Player level, XP, the level-10 bonus loadout slot, and level-gated pads were removed from the design on 2026-09-10; pads gate on completions only.)

**M58 — Character slots, rank display, and mastery** (needs fleshing out)
Extra character slots bought with Diamonds at a rising price or with Robux, and picking which character to bring. Title and rank shown in the Queue Menu and over the head, the recommended rank on each Contract (never a lock), max-level rewards other players can see, and the run-end awards (top miner, gunner, repairer) on the M33 summary. Needs slot prices and the reward values decided first.

**M16 — Mine generator (data only)** ✅
Finished 2026-09-12. Shared `MineGenerator` (pure; config assembled from `GameData/Rocks`, `GameData/Control/Difficulty`, `GameData/Mines/Generation`): the eleven GDD pipeline steps in order, a flat grid of `Enums.RockName` integers (0 = air) in mine-local depth/height/width, resolved chambers, and a report (seed, grid size, per-chamber diggable volume, share within pickaxe reach, requested vs placed ore including hand-placed rock, HP, cumulative uranium). Validation returns fatal and warning lists naming parameter and chamber; `preview` gives expected ore and HP from the file alone; `formatReport` prints one line per chamber. Seeded: nil draws and reports a seed; each chamber uses its own sub-seed so editing one never reshuffles another. Mine files in `GameData/Mines` (`DeadmansCanyon` = GDD worked example, `FrostbitePass` placeholder copy, `Registry` by `Enums.Map`) plus `Enums.MineFacing`. **M62 — Pocket lurkers** ✅
Added 2026-10-06 after the first M34 playtest, when pockets were tripled and made bigger with depth so finding one is a moment; the lurker is what makes the moment a risk. Scope agreed 2026-10-08, as a spider: one pocket in five (from the mine's seed) holds a Cave Spider asleep on its ceiling; the first block broken on the pocket's wall wakes it. It crawls any surface of the mine (every air cell walkable, six ways, no gravity, so no path field and no raycasts), hunts players only, chases anywhere in the mine, by day and by night, never leaves it and never counts toward a wave. Behaviour in three layers: straight down a line of air it can see a player along; a breadth-first search through the air round it, capped at 12 cells, when the line breaks; a drift to a neighbouring cell with nothing to chase. Lethal early (120 HP, 35 damage, three bites on a fresh player) and fine later (designer's pick: run and come back armed). Built: `LurkerManager` (gameplay server) as `MonsterManager`'s crawl stepper, `LurkerRules` (pure, tested: pocket draw, wake shell, resting cell, sight line, bounded search, drift), the `CRAWL` traversal and `CAVE_SPIDER` kind, pockets recorded by the generator, `GameData/Monsters/Lurkers`, the client laying a crawler on the face its cell clings to from its own grid, the dev panel's "Spawn lurker here". Pockets remade the same day as rooms (designer: a floor of at least 4 by 4 blocks, 16 cells, so a spider never wakes right in the player's face; 5 by 5 was tried first and was too big): `MineGenerator.pocketBounds` / `isCellInPocket` define a 4-by-4 room three rows tall with a stepped-in top row, every zone on both mines at radius 2 with counts cut to about 5% air, and the lurker rules read the room's box. The search leaps one open-air cell (a cell touching no rock) at a time, since the cell where a wide tunnel meets a room's wall sealed a spider in. A woken spider starts on the floor cell farthest from the broken block. Spiders only from the designer's zone 3 down (`Lurkers.MIN_ZONE_INDEX`), a fixed 10 for a solo crew plus 2 per extra member, 20 at most, instead of a chance.
